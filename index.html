Bolivia

Cerro Rico, also known as the "Rich Mountain," is a legendary silver mountain located in Potosí, Bolivia. During the Spanish colonial period, it was the world's largest source of silver, fueling the Spanish economy for centuries. The mountain stands at 4,782 meters (15,689 ft) above sea level and has been continuously mined since the 16th century, earning Potosí the status of a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Tragically, millions of indigenous workers andAfrican slaves perished in the harsh mining conditions, leading to the saying that the mountain was made of "silver and blood." Today, while silver deposits are largely depleted, mining continues for other minerals like zinc and tin, though the mountain's structural integrity has been compromised by centuries of extensive tunneling.
